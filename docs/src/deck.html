One block is presented: the PicoRV32 RV32I core on SkyWater sky130, taken to block-level signoff at an 8.25 ns clock in two builds: the commercial build of release v1.0.1 and the open-flow build added in release v2.0, both under the same constraint file and verified with the same open-source scripts. Background: PicoRV32

THE CORE

  • PicoRV32 is a size-optimized RISC-V CPU core by Claire Xenia Wolf, released under the ISC license [1].
  • The core can be configured as RV32E, RV32I, RV32IC, RV32IM or RV32IMC [1].
  • On Xilinx 7-series devices, the core runs at 250–450 MHz in 750–2,000 LUTs, with a CPI of about 4 [1]. These figures and the 40-instruction count are externally sourced background, outside this repository's evidence.
  • PicoRV32 is a general-purpose CPU, and RV32I is the 40-instruction RISC-V base integer ISA [2].

THIS BUILD: DEFAULT PARAMETERS

  • The default parameters are used: RV32I, with 32 registers and counters [1].
  • Multiply/divide, compressed instructions and interrupts are not included [1].
  • Misaligned accesses and illegal instructions cause a trap [1].

Process: the SkyWater SKY130 open 130 nm PDK is used, with sky130_fd_sc_hd cells [3].

[1] github.com/YosysHQ/picorv32 · [2] RISC-V ISA Manual Vol. I (RISC-V International) · [3] github.com/google/skywater-pdk

Background on the core. PicoRV32 is a size-optimized RISC-V CPU core by Claire Xenia Wolf, released under the ISC license [1]. It can be configured as RV32E, RV32I, RV32IC, RV32IM or RV32IMC; on Xilinx 7-series devices it runs at 250 to 450 MHz in 750 to 2,000 LUTs, with a CPI of about 4 [1]. These FPGA figures and the 40-instruction count are externally sourced background, outside this repository's evidence and reproduction guarantee. It is a general-purpose CPU, and RV32I is the 40-instruction RISC-V base integer ISA [2]. The default parameters are used in this build: RV32I, with 32 registers and counters. Multiply and divide, compressed instructions and interrupts are not included, and misaligned accesses and illegal instructions cause a trap [1]. The process is the SkyWater SKY130 open 130 nm PDK with sky130_fd_sc_hd cells [3]. On the limitations slide, "one 9-instruction program" means one short program, not one instruction and not one ISA. The full 40-instruction RV32I is implemented by the core, and 6 of those opcodes are exercised by the program. Formal equivalence checking concerns the configured design; static timing analysis covers the paths constrained by the shipped SDC, not every interface. Sources: [1] github.com/YosysHQ/picorv32; [2] RISC-V ISA Manual Vol. I, RISC-V International; [3] github.com/google/skywater-pdk.

04 · TRACE-V · PicoRV32 on SKY130

The block is PicoRV32, an RV32I RISC-V core by Claire Xenia Wolf under the ISC license, implemented on SkyWater sky130A with the sky130_fd_sc_hd standard cells. The clock is 8.25 ns, 121 MHz, with 0.25 ns of clock uncertainty at every corner. The three corners are ss at 100 degrees C and 1.60 V, tt at 25 degrees C and 1.80 V, and ff at minus 40 degrees C and 1.95 V. Implementation uses commercial tools: Genus for synthesis, Innovus for place and route, Tempus for timing and SDF, Conformal for logic equivalence and Voltus for IR. Physical signoff uses open checkers so that the results can be re-verified by anyone: Magic DRC, KLayout DRC with the sky130A_mr deck, Magic antenna, LVS with Magic and Netgen, and functional gate-level simulation with partial SDF timing (Icarus Verilog); CVC checks the SDF timing checks. The 87,237 transistors are counted from the sky130 SPICE models of every placed cell. Fillers, taps and antenna diodes add no transistors.

MODIFIED OPEN-SOURCE TOOLS

Where the open-source tools were changed

OpenROAD Resizer: repair

After routing, electrical and timing repair use the extracted parasitics, including zero-length shunt capacitances. The replaced estimate under-counted wire load 2.2 to 2.4 times on the nets of a parasitic probe (a development record, not released here). Driver sizing uses the corner model and the SDC slew limit.

OpenSTA in OpenROAD: parasitics

The SPEF reader and parasitic store give the Resizer the extracted RC network in a form its buffering model can use; constraint checks ignore empty exception entries.

OpenROAD PSM: static IR

Voltage sources can sit on each power pin's own routing layer as well as on the top layer.

LibreLane: flow integration

A post-route loop repairs electrical, setup and hold violations against fresh extraction, and the flow fails when a stated setup target is missed.

Yosys and ABC, Magic, KLayout, Netgen and Icarus Verilog were not modified. The changes are private and not published.

15 · TRACE-V · PicoRV32 on SKY130

The guarantees of the method are described here, without implementation detail. One: the requirements were set once by the designer, and each decision was approved by the designer. The requirements are 0.25 ns uncertainty at ss, tt and ff, DRC clean in two independent decks, slew, capacitance and fanout at 0, gate-level simulation and LEC passing, and IR within budget. Synthesis, place and route, signoff and debugging were carried out by agentic AI against these requirements. Two: figures cite the run that produced them, and the released evidence files carry provenance records. Three: the historical runs were frozen with checksum manifests. A reconstruction from eight manifests gives 75 entries, 66 in the six implementation and verification sets plus Voltus and 9 in the Xcelium set; a checksum check of the original files on 16 October 2026 checked 75 entries with 0 mismatches. This is a reconstruction and a new readback, not a figure the project stated at the time. Four: key checks were run on a known-bad input. Examples are gate-level simulation failing at 4 ns, the antenna check failing on a known-bad layout, and the slew, capacitance and fanout check flagging a deliberately overloaded net with a 5 pF load. These show the stated responses in those runs, not that every checker detects every damaged input. Five: a check that cannot run is recorded as a failure. Six: layouts built by Innovus are checked by Magic, KLayout and Netgen, and LEC links the routed netlist back to the RTL, so that the output of each tool is checked by a different tool.

Equivalence of the open build: three setup steps

Yosys re-encodes state machines and leaves registers that the default configuration never uses. A plain key-point comparison against the RTL cannot prove equivalence. Conformal LEC 24.10 proves it after three steps.

STEP 1

Flip-flops renamed after their RTL registers.

The renaming script asserts that cells and connections are identical.

RESULT

Names change; cells and connections stay identical.

STEP 2

FSM encoding files for two state registers.

cpu_state: 8-bit one-hot to 7-bit one-hot. mem_wordsize: 2-bit binary to 3-bit one-hot.

CONTROL

Without these files, 1,246 points are non-equivalent (setup record).

STEP 3

Conformal setup analysis.

77 RTL registers were merged (duplicates and registers the default configuration never uses), and 5 were treated as constants.

RESULT

Equivalent: 1,959 points, 0 non-equivalent, aborted or unknown.

The Yosys netlist is also equivalent to the routed netlist of the final layout: 1,904 points, 0 non-equivalent.
